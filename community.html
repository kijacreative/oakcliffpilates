01Outdoors

Pilates in the Park.

Mat classes on the grass, open to anyone who turns up. It is the format Oak Cliff Pilates began with in 2016, when Amanda taught free classes at Kidd Springs Park for whoever wanted to come, and it is still the easiest way to try us without setting foot in a studio.

We have moved on from Kidd Springs, but not from the idea — these are the parks you will find us in now.

No reformer, no membership, no experience needed. Bring a mat and a bottle of water.

Nights & socials.

Four recurring formats. Some are classes, some are just a table and good company.

A few times a year

Gentlemen’s Night

We hand the reformers to the men — a class with Zak, Oliver or Pepe, specialty cocktails, and a proper hang afterwards. Bring the one in your life who still says Pilates isn’t for him.

Monthly · Fridays

Community Happy Hour

Caitlin takes a group of members and friends to a neighbourhood bar or restaurant for drinks, snacks and introductions. We’re working our way through every good place near the studios.

Monthly · Uptown

Book Club

There’s a book nook at the Uptown studio, run by Amanda Lauro of Forbidden Books. One book a month, snacks, wine, and an actual conversation. Our newest community, and growing.

Monthly

Athena

For women in perimenopause or menopause — and for anyone with someone in their life going through it. Monthly events pairing real education with real company.

We use a couple of cookies.

Two small ones, both ours: one remembers this choice, one remembers you have already seen the intro offer. Booking and checkout run on Arketa and set their own. No analytics, no advertising, nothing that follows you to other sites.

Cookie preferences

Strictly necessary Always on

The site cannot work without these, so there is no switch. None of them identify you or leave this site.

  • ocp_consent — remembers what you choose here. 12 months.
  • ocp_popup_seen — so the intro offer shows once, not on every page. 30 days.
  • Arketa — our booking and payment system, embedded on the schedule, shop and checkout pages. It sets its own cookies so it can hold your basket and keep you signed in. Blocking them would mean you could not book or pay.
Video

Videos in the Journal are served by YouTube. Nothing loads until you press play, and we use their no-cookie domain — but pressing play still contacts Google, which may store data. Leave this off and we will ask before any video loads.
